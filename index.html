What is Specialty Coffee?

Specialty coffee refers to the highest grade of coffee available, typically grown at high altitudes, cultivated with care, and roasted to highlight unique regional characteristics.

The Origin Story

Unlike commercial coffee, specialty beans are fully traceable to specific farms or micro-lots, ensuring ethical trade and superior quality control from seed to cup.

Popular Manual Brewing Methods

Brewing specialty coffee is both a science and an art. The extraction method significantly influences the taste profile of the final cup.

  1. Pour-Over (V60 / Chemex): Highlights bright acidity, floral notes, and clean body.
  2. French Press: Emphasizes full body, rich oils, and deep chocolatey notes.
  3. Aeropress: Offers high versatility, allowing for concentrated shot-like brews or smooth immersion cups.
  4. Espresso: Uses high pressure to extract a rich, dense shot topped with natural crema.

Understanding Flavor Profiles

Acidity & Brightness

High-altitude Arabica coffee often exhibits crisp fruit acidity, reminiscent of citrus, berries, or green apples.

Sweetness & Body

Natural sugars caramelize during roasting, creating rich sweetness reminiscent of cane sugar, honey, or milk chocolate.
